Joab, the son of Zeruiah, was King David's nephew and the commander of his army. He first appears in [[1Sam.26.6|1 Samuel 26:6]], accompanying David on a mission to Saul's camp. Joab played a significant role in David's rise to power and the establishment of his kingdom \([[2Sam.2|2Sa.2-3]]; [[2Sam.8.16|8.16]]; [[1Chr.11.6|1Ch.11.6]],[[1Chr.11.8|8]]\). 

As a military commander, Joab led David's forces in various battles against the Ammonites, Syrians, and other enemies \([[2Sam.10|2Sa.10]]; [[2Sam.12.26|12.26-27]]; [[1Chr.19|1Ch.19]]\). He also helped quell Absalom's rebellion and was instrumental in defeating Sheba's revolt \([[2Sam.18|2Sa.18]]; [[2Sam.20|20]]\). 

However, Joab's loyalty to David was sometimes overshadowed by his own ambition and ruthlessness. He killed Abner, Saul's former commander, in revenge for his brother Asahel's death \([[2Sam.3|2Sa.3]]\). He also disobeyed David's orders by killing Absalom \([[2Sam.18|2Sa.18]]\) and supported Adonijah's attempt to seize the throne \([[1Kgs.1.7|1Ki.1.7]],[[1Kgs.1.19|19]]\). 

Despite his flaws, Joab remained a key figure in David's reign until the end. He was involved in the census that brought God's judgment upon Israel \([[2Sam.24|2Sa.24]]; [[1Chr.21|1Ch.21]]\) and was eventually killed by Benaiah on Solomon's orders for his support of Adonijah \([[1Kgs.2.28|1Ki.2.28-35]]\).
