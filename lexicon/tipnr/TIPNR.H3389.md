Jerusalem is one of the most important cities in the Bible, serving as the capital of Israel and a holy city for Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. It is mentioned hundreds of times throughout the Old and New Testaments. The city was originally known as Salem \([[Gen.14.18|Genesis 14:18]]; [[Ps.76.2|Psalm 76:2]]\) and was later called Jebus, after the Jebusites who inhabited it \([[Judg.19.10|Judges 19:10]]; [[1Chr.11.4|1 Chronicles 11:4]]\). David conquered the city and made it his capital \([[2Sam.5.6|2 Samuel 5:6-9]]; [[1Chr.11.4|1 Chronicles 11:4-9]]\). Solomon built the first temple in Jerusalem \([[1Kgs.6|1 Kings 6-8]]; [[2Chr.3|2 Chronicles 3-7]]\), which became the center of Jewish worship. The city was destroyed by the Babylonians in 586 BC \([[2Kgs.25.1|2 Kings 25:1-10]]; [[2Chr.36.17|2 Chronicles 36:17-19]]\) but was later rebuilt after the exile \([[Ezra.1.1|Ezra 1:1-4]]; [[Neh.2.11|Nehemiah 2:11-20]]\). In the New Testament, Jerusalem is the site of many events in the life of Jesus, including his triumphal entry \([[Matt.21.1|Matthew 21:1-11]]\), cleansing of the temple \([[Matt.21.12|Matthew 21:12-17]]\), and crucifixion \([[Matt.27.32|Matthew 27:32-56]]\). The early church also began in Jerusalem \([[Acts.1|Acts 1-7]]\). Throughout the Bible, Jerusalem is referred to by various names, each highlighting a different aspect of its significance: Ariel \([[Isa.29.1|Isaiah 29:1]]\), My Delight Is in Her \([[Isa.62.4|Isaiah 62:4]]\), Forsaken and Desolate \([[Isa.62.4|Isaiah 62:4]]\), Married \([[Isa.62.4|Isaiah 62:4]]\), the City of the Lord \([[Isa.60.14|Isaiah 60:14]]\), Zion \([[Ps.87.2|Psalm 87:2]]\), and Mount Zion \([[Ps.48.1|Psalm 48:1]]\). The name "Jerusalem" itself may mean "foundation of peace" or "possession of peace."
