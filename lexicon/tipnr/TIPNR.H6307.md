Paddan, also referred to as Paddan-aram or Aram, was a region in Mesopotamia that played a significant role in the lives of the biblical patriarchs. It was the homeland of Abraham's family, and the place where Isaac and Israel \(or 'Jacob'\) found their wives \([[Gen.25.20|Gen. 25:20]]; [[Gen.28.2|28:2]], [[Gen.28.5|5-7]]\). The name "Paddan-aram" means "the plain of Aram" or "the field of Aram," referring to the area's geographical characteristics and its association with the Arameans. 

Rebekah, Isaac's wife, was from Paddan-aram \([[Gen.25.20|Gen. 25:20]]\). Later, Jacob fled to Paddan-aram to escape his brother Esau's anger and to find a wife from among his relatives \([[Gen.28.2|Gen. 28:2]], [[Gen.28.5|5-7]]\). During his time there, Jacob married Leah and Rachel and fathered eleven of his twelve sons \([[Gen.35.23|Gen. 35:23-26]]\). Jacob eventually left Paddan-aram to return to Canaan with his family and possessions \([[Gen.31.18|Gen. 31:18]]; [[Gen.33.18|33:18]]\). 

The Arameans, who inhabited Paddan-aram, are mentioned as the ancestors of the Israelites in the context of God's deliverance and blessing \([[Deut.26.5|Deut. 26:5]]\). The prophet Hosea also refers to Jacob's flight to Paddan-aram and his service for a wife \([[Hos.12.12|Hos. 12:12]]\).
