Ephraim was the second son of Joseph, born to him in Egypt by his wife Asenath \([[Gen.41.52|Gen.41.52]]\). He and his older brother Manasseh were adopted by Israel \(or 'Jacob'\) and blessed, with Ephraim receiving the greater blessing \([[Gen.48|Gen.48]]\). Ephraim's descendants formed one of the twelve tribes of Israel, and they were often grouped with the tribe of Manasseh under the collective name "Joseph" \([[Num.1.32|Num.1.32]],[[Num.1.33|33]]; [[Num.26.28|26.28]],[[Num.26.35|35]],[[Num.26.37|37]]\). 

The tribe of Ephraim settled in the central region of Canaan \([[Josh.16.4|Jos.16.4]],[[Josh.16.5|5]],[[Josh.16.8|8]],[[Josh.16.9|9]],[[Josh.16.10|10]]\) and played a significant role throughout Israel's history. They were known for their military prowess \([[Judg.5.14|Jdg.5.14]]; [[Ps.78.9|Psa.78.9]]\) and produced several notable leaders, including Joshua \([[Num.13.8|Num.13.8]]\), Abdon \([[Judg.12.15|Jdg.12.15]]\), and Samuel \([[1Sam.1.1|1Sa.1.1]]\). However, they also exhibited pride and a rebellious spirit at times \([[Judg.8.1|Jdg.8.1]],[[Judg.8.2|2]]; [[Judg.12.1|12.1]]; [[2Sam.20.21|2Sa.20.21]]\). 

Ephraim, along with the other northern tribes, separated from Judah after Solomon's reign and formed the northern kingdom of Israel \([[1Kgs.12.25|1Ki.12.25]]\). The prophets often addressed Ephraim, warning them of judgment for their idolatry and unfaithfulness \([[Isa.7.2|Isa.7.2]],[[Isa.7.5|5]],[[Isa.7.8|8]],[[Isa.7.9|9]]; [[Jer.31.18|Jer.31.18]],[[Jer.31.20|20]]; [[Hos.4.17|Hos.4.17]]; [[Hos.4.5|5]]\). However, there were also promises of restoration and blessing \([[Jer.31.6|Jer.31.6]],[[Jer.31.9|9]]; [[Ezek.37.16|Ezk.37.16]],[[Ezek.37.19|19]]\). 

In the New Testament, Ephraim is mentioned in the context of Jesus withdrawing to a village called Ephraim before his final entry into Jerusalem \([[Judg.12.6|Jdg.12.6]]; [[1Chr.12.30|1Ch.12.30]]; [[1Chr.27.20|27.20]]; [[2Chr.25.7|2Ch.25.7]]; [[Ps.78.9|Psa.78.9]]; [[2Chr.13.19|2Ch.13.19]]; [[1Kgs.11.26|1Ki.11.26]]; [[1Sam.1.1|1Sa.1.1]]\).
