Levite living at the time of the New Testament

<span class='tipnrhighlight'>Mark</span> \(also called John\), Barnabas' cousin and Paul's companion, was the author of the Gospel of Mark \([[Acts.12.12|Act.12.12]],[[Acts.12.25|25]]; [[Acts.15.37|15.37]],[[Acts.15.39|39]]; [[Col.4.10|Col.4.10]]; [[2Tim.4.11|2Ti.4.11]]; [[Phlm.1.24|Phm.1.24]]; [[1Pet.5.13|1Pe.5.13]]; [[Acts.13.5|Act.13.5]],[[Acts.13.13|13]]; [[Acts.15.37|15.37]]\).
- A man living at the time of the New Testament, first mentioned at [[Acts.12.12|Act.12.12]]; 
- referred to as Mark **<big>[[G3138|Μάρκος]]</big>**, or John **<big>[[G2491|Ἰωάννης]]</big>**; 
- his mother was Mary **<big>[[G3137|Μαρία]]</big>**.

Mark, also known as John Mark, was a prominent figure in the early Christian church. He was the son of Mary, whose house in Jerusalem served as a meeting place for the believers \([[Acts.12.12|Acts 12:12]]\). Mark was also the cousin of Barnabas \([[Col.4.10|Colossians 4:10]]\) and accompanied Paul and Barnabas on their first missionary journey \([[Acts.12.25|Acts 12:25]]; [[Acts.13.5|13:5]]\). However, he left them at Perga and returned to Jerusalem \([[Acts.13.13|Acts 13:13]]\), which later caused a disagreement between Paul and Barnabas \([[Acts.15.37|Acts 15:37-39]]\). Despite this, Mark remained active in ministry, as evidenced by Paul's later references to him as a fellow worker \([[Phlm.1.24|Philemon 1:24]]; [[2Tim.4.11|2 Timothy 4:11]]\). He also worked closely with Peter, who referred to him as "my son Mark" \([[1Pet.5.13|1 Peter 5:13]]\). Tradition holds that Mark wrote the Gospel that bears his name based on Peter's teachings, making it one of the earliest written accounts of Jesus' life and ministry.
