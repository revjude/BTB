Jehoiakim, also known as Eliakim, was a king of Judah who reigned for eleven years \(609-598 BC\). He was the second son of King Josiah and became king after his younger brother Jehoahaz was deposed by Pharaoh Necho II of Egypt \([[2Kgs.23.34|2 Kings 23:34]]; [[2Chr.36.4|2 Chronicles 36:4]]\). Necho changed Eliakim's name to Jehoiakim and imposed a heavy tribute on Judah \([[2Kgs.23.35|2 Kings 23:35]]\). 

During Jehoiakim's reign, Judah became a vassal state of Egypt and later Babylon \([[2Kgs.24.1|2 Kings 24:1]]\). Jehoiakim initially served Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon but later rebelled against him \([[2Kgs.24.1|2 Kings 24:1]]\). This rebellion led to the first Babylonian invasion of Judah, during which some of the temple treasures and members of the royal family and nobility were taken to Babylon \([[2Chr.36.6|2 Chronicles 36:6-7]]; [[Dan.1.1|Daniel 1:1-2]]\). 

The prophet Jeremiah prophesied during Jehoiakim's reign, warning the king and the people of Judah about the impending Babylonian judgment \([[Jer.1.3|Jeremiah 1:3]]; [[Jer.25.1|25:1]]; [[Jer.26.1|26:1]]\). Jehoiakim opposed Jeremiah, burning the scroll containing the prophet's warnings \([[Jer.36|Jeremiah 36]]\) and persecuting those who followed Jeremiah's message \([[Jer.26.20|Jeremiah 26:20-23]]\). 

Jehoiakim died before the final Babylonian invasion and was succeeded by his son Jehoiachin \([[2Kgs.24.6|2 Kings 24:6]]\). The circumstances of his death are not clearly stated in the biblical text, but Jeremiah prophesied that he would be buried like a donkey, dragged and thrown outside the gates of Jerusalem \([[Jer.22.18|Jeremiah 22:18-19]]; [[Jer.36.30|36:30]]\).
