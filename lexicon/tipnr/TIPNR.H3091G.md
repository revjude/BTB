Joshua, the son of Nun, was a prominent figure in the Old Testament who served as Moses' assistant and later succeeded him as the leader of the Israelites. He was from the tribe of Ephraim \([[Num.13.8|Numbers 13:8]]\) and was originally named Hoshea, but Moses changed his name to Joshua \([[Num.13.16|Numbers 13:16]]\). Joshua was one of the twelve spies sent to explore the land of Canaan \([[Num.13.8|Numbers 13:8]]\), and he and Caleb were the only ones who brought back a positive report and encouraged the people to trust God \([[Num.14.6|Numbers 14:6-9]]\). As a result, Joshua and Caleb were the only adults from their generation permitted to enter the Promised Land \([[Num.14.30|Numbers 14:30]], [[Num.14.38|38]]\). 

Before Moses' death, God appointed Joshua to succeed him as the leader of Israel \([[Num.27.18|Numbers 27:18-23]]; [[Deut.31.7|Deuteronomy 31:7-8]], [[Deut.31.14|14]], [[Deut.31.23|23]]\). After Moses' death, Joshua led the Israelites across the Jordan River and into the land of Canaan \([[Josh.1|Joshua 1-4]]\). He oversaw the conquest of Jericho \([[Josh.6|Joshua 6]]\), Ai \([[Josh.8|Joshua 8]]\), and numerous other cities and territories \([[Josh.10|Joshua 10-12]]\). Joshua also led the Israelites in renewing their covenant with God \([[Josh.8.30|Joshua 8:30-35]]; [[Josh.24.1|24:1-28]]\). 

Under Joshua's leadership, the land of Canaan was divided among the tribes of Israel as their inheritance \([[Josh.13|Joshua 13-21]]\). In his old age, Joshua gave a farewell address to the Israelites, encouraging them to remain faithful to God \([[Josh.23|Joshua 23-24]]\). He died at the age of 110 and was buried in his inheritance at Timnath-serah \([[Josh.24.29|Joshua 24:29-30]]; [[Judg.2.8|Judges 2:8-9]]\). 

Joshua is remembered as a courageous leader, a faithful servant of God, and a key figure in Israel's history who led the people into the Promised Land and helped establish them as a nation.
