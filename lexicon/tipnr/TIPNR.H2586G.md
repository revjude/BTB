Hanun was the king of the Ammonites during the reign of King David of Israel. He is mentioned in [[2Sam.10|2 Samuel 10]] and [[1Chr.19|1 Chronicles 19]]. After the death of Hanun's father, Nahash, who had shown kindness to David, David sent envoys to express his sympathy to Hanun \([[2Sam.10.1|2 Samuel 10:1-2]]; [[1Chr.19.1|1 Chronicles 19:1-2]]\). However, Hanun's advisors convinced him that David's envoys were spies, so Hanun humiliated them by shaving off half of their beards and cutting their garments short \([[2Sam.10.3|2 Samuel 10:3-4]]; [[1Chr.19.3|1 Chronicles 19:3-4]]\). This act was a grave insult and a violation of diplomatic protocol. When David heard about this, he was outraged and prepared for war against the Ammonites \([[2Sam.10.5|2 Samuel 10:5-7]]; [[1Chr.19.6|1 Chronicles 19:6-7]]\). Hanun, realizing his mistake, hired Syrian mercenaries to help him fight against Israel \([[2Sam.10.6|2 Samuel 10:6]]; [[1Chr.19.6|1 Chronicles 19:6-7]]\). However, the Israelite army, led by Joab and Abishai, defeated the Ammonites and their Syrian allies \([[2Sam.10.8|2 Samuel 10:8-14]]; [[1Chr.19.8|1 Chronicles 19:8-15]]\). The conflict continued, and David himself later joined the battle, capturing the Ammonite capital, Rabbah \([[2Sam.12.26|2 Samuel 12:26-31]]; [[1Chr.20.1|1 Chronicles 20:1-3]]\). Hanun's mistreatment of David's envoys and the resulting war highlight the importance of diplomatic relations and the consequences of disrespecting allies in the ancient Near East.
