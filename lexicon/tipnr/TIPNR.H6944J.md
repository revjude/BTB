<small>*Holy Place\_2 • The inner sanctuary of the Tabernacle and Temple*</small>

The <span class='tipnrhighlight'>Holy Place</span> was the inner sanctuary of the Tabernacle and Temple.
- A location in Jerusalem **<big>[[H3389|יְרוּשָׁלַ͏ִם]]</big>** first mentioned at [[Exod.26.33|Exo.26.33]]; 
- referred to as Holy\_Place or Holy Place **<big>[[H6944|קֹדֶשׁ]]</big>**, or Holy Place **<big>[[G0039|ἅγιον]]</big>**.<br>

The Holy Place refers to the inner sanctuary of the Tabernacle and later the Temple, where the priests performed their duties. It was separated from the outer court by a veil \([[Exod.26.33|Exodus 26:33]]\). The Holy Place contained the lampstand, the table of showbread, and the altar of incense \([[Exod.31.8|Exodus 31:8]]; [[Exod.35.19|35:19]]; [[Exod.39.1|39:1]]\). Only consecrated priests could enter the Holy Place \([[Exod.28.29|Exodus 28:29]],[[Exod.28.43|43]]; [[Exod.29.30|29:30]]\). Rituals performed there included burning incense and presenting offerings \([[Lev.6.30|Leviticus 6:30]]; [[Num.28.7|Numbers 28:7]]\). The Holy Place represented God's presence among His people, though access was restricted. In the New Testament, Christ's sacrifice opened the way for believers to enter God's presence \([[Heb.9.12|Hebrews 9:12]]; [[Heb.10.19|10:19]]\).
