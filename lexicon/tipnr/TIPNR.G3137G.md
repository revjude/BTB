Mary, the mother of Jesus, was a young Jewish woman from Nazareth betrothed to Joseph, a descendant of David. Before their marriage, an angel appeared to Mary, announcing that she would conceive a child by the Holy Spirit and name Him Jesus. Mary humbly accepted this divine plan, and the virgin birth of Jesus fulfilled the prophecy of the Messiah \([[Matt.1.16|Matthew 1:16]], [[Matt.1.18|18]], [[Matt.1.20|20]]; [[Luke.1.26|Luke 1:26-38]]\). After Jesus' birth in Bethlehem, Mary and Joseph presented Him at the temple, where Simeon prophesied that a sword would pierce her soul \([[Luke.2.5|Luke 2:5]], [[Luke.2.16|16]], [[Luke.2.19|19]], [[Luke.2.34|34]]\). Mary treasured these events in her heart. She and Joseph raised Jesus in Nazareth, and He was known as the son of Mary \([[Matt.13.55|Matthew 13:55]]; [[Mark.6.3|Mark 6:3]]\). During Jesus' crucifixion, Mary stood near the cross with other women, including her sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene \([[Matt.27.56|Matthew 27:56]], [[Matt.27.61|61]]; [[Mark.15.40|Mark 15:40]], [[Mark.15.47|47]]\). After Jesus' resurrection, Mary was among the women who went to the tomb and later informed the disciples \([[Matt.28.1|Matthew 28:1]]; [[Mark.16.1|Mark 16:1]]; [[Luke.24.10|Luke 24:10]]\). In the early church, Mary joined the disciples in prayer as they awaited the coming of the Holy Spirit \([[Acts.1.14|Acts 1:14]]\).
