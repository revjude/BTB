Boaz was a wealthy man from Bethlehem who played a significant role in the story of Ruth \([[Ruth.2|Ruth 2-4]]\). He was a relative of Naomi's deceased husband, Elimelech, and acted as a kinsman-redeemer for Ruth, a Moabite widow. When Ruth gleaned grain in Boaz's field, he showed her kindness and protection, recognizing her loyalty to Naomi \([[Ruth.2|Ruth 2]]\). Later, Naomi instructed Ruth to approach Boaz at night and request that he fulfill his role as a kinsman-redeemer \([[Ruth.3|Ruth 3]]\). Boaz agreed to marry Ruth, after ensuring that a closer relative did not wish to claim this right \([[Ruth.4.1|Ruth 4:1-12]]\). Their marriage led to the birth of Obed, who became the grandfather of King David \([[Ruth.4.13|Ruth 4:13-17]]\). Boaz is also mentioned in the genealogy of Jesus Christ \([[Matt.1.5|Matt. 1:5]]; [[Luke.3.32|Luke 3:32]]\), highlighting his importance in God's plan of redemption. Additionally, Boaz is associated with one of the two bronze pillars in Solomon's temple, which was named after him \([[1Kgs.7.21|1 Kings 7:21]]; [[1Chr.2.11|1 Chr. 2:11-12]]\). His story demonstrates the importance of integrity, kindness, and faithfulness in fulfilling one's duties and God's purposes.
