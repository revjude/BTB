Jonah was a prophet from Gath-hepher during the reign of Jeroboam II, king of Israel \([[2Kgs.14.25|2 Kings 14:25]]\). God called Jonah to preach against the wickedness of Nineveh, the capital of Assyria \([[Jonah.1.1|Jonah 1:1-2]]\). Jonah fled by ship in the opposite direction to Tarshish \([[Jonah.1.3|Jonah 1:3]]\). A storm arose and the sailors, discovering it was Jonah's fault, threw him overboard \([[Jonah.1.4|Jonah 1:4-15]]\). God provided a great fish to swallow Jonah, and he was in its belly for three days and nights \([[Jonah.1.17|Jonah 1:17]]\). Jonah prayed, and the fish vomited him onto dry land \([[Jonah.2.1|Jonah 2:1-10]]\). God again told Jonah to preach to Nineveh \([[Jonah.3.1|Jonah 3:1-2]]\). Jonah obeyed, and the people repented \([[Jonah.3.3|Jonah 3:3-9]]\). God relented of the disaster he had threatened \([[Jonah.3.10|Jonah 3:10]]\). Jonah was angry that God spared the city \([[Jonah.4.1|Jonah 4:1-3]]\). God caused a plant to shade Jonah, then made it wither \([[Jonah.4.5|Jonah 4:5-8]]\). God used this to rebuke Jonah's lack of pity for Nineveh \([[Jonah.4.9|Jonah 4:9-11]]\). Jesus compared his future burial and resurrection to Jonah's time in the fish \([[Matt.12.39|Matthew 12:39-41]], [[Matt.16.4|16:4]]; [[Luke.11.29|Luke 11:29-32]]\).
