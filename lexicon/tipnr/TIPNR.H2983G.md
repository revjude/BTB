The Jebusites were one of the Canaanite tribes that inhabited the land promised to the Israelites by God. They are first mentioned in [[Gen.10.16|Genesis 10:16]] and 15:21 as descendants of Canaan, the grandson of Noah. When the Israelites left Egypt, God promised to drive out the Jebusites and other Canaanite tribes from the land \([[Exod.3.8|Exodus 3:8]], [[Exod.3.17|17]]; [[Exod.13.5|13:5]]; [[Exod.23.23|23:23]]; [[Exod.33.2|33:2]]; [[Exod.34.11|34:11]]\). During the conquest of Canaan under Joshua, the Jebusites were among the tribes that the Israelites were commanded to utterly destroy \([[Deut.7.1|Deuteronomy 7:1]]; [[Deut.20.17|20:17]]\). However, the Jebusites proved to be formidable opponents, and the Israelites were unable to completely drive them out of Jerusalem \([[Josh.15.63|Joshua 15:63]]; [[Judg.1.21|Judges 1:21]]\). The Jebusites continued to live among the Israelites during the time of the judges \([[Judg.3.5|Judges 3:5]]; [[Judg.19.11|19:11]]\). It was not until the reign of King David that the Jebusites were finally defeated, and Jerusalem was captured and made the capital of Israel \([[2Sam.5.6|2 Samuel 5:6-8]]; [[1Chr.11.4|1 Chronicles 11:4-6]]\). After their defeat, some Jebusites remained in the land and were subjected to forced labor by Solomon \([[1Kgs.9.20|1 Kings 9:20]]; [[2Chr.8.7|2 Chronicles 8:7]]\). The site where the temple was built, Mount Moriah, was purchased by David from Araunah the Jebusite \([[2Sam.24.16|2 Samuel 24:16-18]]; [[1Chr.21.15|1 Chronicles 21:15-28]]; [[2Chr.3.1|2 Chronicles 3:1]]\). The Jebusites are mentioned among the foreign peoples with whom the Israelites intermarried, contrary to God's command \([[Ezra.9.1|Ezra 9:1]]\). In [[Zech.9.7|Zechariah 9:7]], there is a prophetic promise that the Jebusites will be incorporated into God's people, symbolizing the inclusion of Gentiles in the future messianic kingdom.
