David, the son of Jesse from Bethlehem, was a prominent figure in the Old Testament and is mentioned extensively throughout the Bible. He was anointed by Samuel to be king over Israel, succeeding Saul \([[1Sam.16.13|1 Samuel 16:13]]\). David first gained fame as a young man by defeating the Philistine giant Goliath with a sling and a stone \([[1Sam.17|1 Samuel 17]]\). 

David's reign as king was marked by military successes, as he defeated the enemies of Israel and expanded the kingdom \([[2Sam.8|2 Samuel 8]]\). He is known for his devotion to God and is called "a man after God's own heart" \([[1Sam.13.14|1 Samuel 13:14]]; [[Acts.13.22|Acts 13:22]]\). Many of the psalms in the Bible are attributed to David, reflecting his deep faith and trust in God \(e.g., [[Ps.3|Psalms 3]], [[Ps.23|23]], [[Ps.51|51]]\). 

However, David's life was not without flaws. He committed adultery with Bathsheba and orchestrated the death of her husband, Uriah \([[2Sam.11|2 Samuel 11]]\). As a result, David faced consequences, including the death of his child and turmoil within his family \([[2Sam.12|2 Samuel 12-18]]\). 

Despite his failings, David remained a central figure in Israel's history and a model of repentance and faith. He was promised an enduring dynasty, and the Messiah would come from his lineage \([[2Sam.7.12|2 Samuel 7:12-16]]\). This prophecy was fulfilled in Jesus Christ, who is called the "Son of David" \([[Matt.1.1|Matthew 1:1]]; [[Rom.1.3|Romans 1:3]]\). 

David's life and reign are recorded in 1 and 2 Samuel, 1 Chronicles, and portions of 1 Kings. His significance extends into the New Testament, where he is mentioned in connection with Jesus' lineage and messianic identity \([[Matt.1.1|Matthew 1:1]]; [[Luke.1.32|Luke 1:32]]; [[Acts.13.22|Acts 13:22-23]]; [[Rev.22.16|Revelation 22:16]]\).
