Levi was the third son of Israel \(or 'Jacob'\) and Leah \([[Gen.29.34|Gen.29.34]]\). Along with his brother Simeon, he took revenge on the Shechemites for the rape of their sister Dinah \([[Gen.34.25|Gen.34.25-31]]\). Jacob rebuked them for this act on his deathbed \([[Gen.49.5|Gen.49.5-7]]\). Levi had three sons: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari \([[Gen.46.11|Gen.46.11]]; [[Exod.6.16|Exo.6.16]]\). His descendants, the Levites, were set apart as the priestly tribe \([[Exod.32.26|Exo.32.26-29]]; [[Num.3.5|Num.3.5-13]]\). They were responsible for the tabernacle and its services \([[Num.1.47|Num.1.47-54]]; [[Num.3.14|3.14-39]]; [[Num.4.1|4.1-49]]; [[Num.8.5|8.5-26]]\). The Levites were given cities throughout Israel, but no territorial inheritance \([[Num.35.1|Num.35.1-8]]; [[Josh.21.1|Jos.21.1-42]]\). Notable Levites include Moses, Aaron, and Samuel. The Levites are frequently mentioned in the historical and prophetic books of the Old Testament, as well as in the genealogies of 1 Chronicles. In the New Testament, Levites are mentioned in connection with the temple and its services \([[Luke.10.32|Lk.10.32]]; [[John.1.19|Jn.1.19]]; [[Acts.4.36|Act.4.36]]\). The Levitical priesthood is contrasted with the superior priesthood of Christ \([[Heb.7.1|Heb.7.1-28]]\).
