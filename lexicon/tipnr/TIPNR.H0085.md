Abraham, initially named Abram, was a central figure in the early history of the Israelites. He was born in Ur of the Chaldeans and was the son of Terah \([[Gen.11.26|Genesis 11:26-31]]\). God called Abram to leave his country and family and go to a land that He would show him, promising to make him a great nation and to bless all peoples through him \([[Gen.12.1|Genesis 12:1-3]]\). 

Abram obeyed God and journeyed to Canaan with his wife Sarai and his nephew Lot \([[Gen.12.4|Genesis 12:4-5]]\). God reaffirmed His covenant with Abram, promising him numerous descendants and the land of Canaan \([[Gen.13.14|Genesis 13:14-17]]; [[Gen.15.1|15:1-21]]\). God changed Abram's name to Abraham, meaning "father of many," and Sarai's name to Sarah \([[Gen.17.1|Genesis 17:1-16]]\). 

Despite their advanced age, Abraham and Sarah miraculously bore a son, Isaac, through whom God's covenant would be established \([[Gen.18.1|Genesis 18:1-15]]; [[Gen.21.1|21:1-7]]\). Abraham demonstrated his faith and obedience when God tested him by asking him to sacrifice Isaac \([[Gen.22.1|Genesis 22:1-19]]\). God provided a ram as a substitute, reaffirming His promise to Abraham \([[Gen.22.15|Genesis 22:15-18]]\). 

Abraham's faith was credited to him as righteousness \([[Gen.15.6|Genesis 15:6]]; [[Rom.4.3|Romans 4:3]]; [[Gal.3.6|Galatians 3:6]]; [[Jas.2.23|James 2:23]]\). He became known as the father of the faithful and the friend of God \([[Isa.41.8|Isaiah 41:8]]; [[2Chr.20.7|2 Chronicles 20:7]]; [[Jas.2.23|James 2:23]]\). God's covenant with Abraham, which included the promise of land, descendants, and blessing, was passed down to his son Isaac and grandson Israel \(or 'Jacob'\) \([[Gen.26.1|Genesis 26:1-5]]; [[Gen.28.10|28:10-15]]\). 

The life and faith of Abraham are significant in both the Old and New Testaments. He is recognized as the ancestor of the Israelites \([[Exod.2.24|Exodus 2:24]]; [[Exod.3.6|3:6]], [[Exod.3.15|15-16]]; [[Exod.4.5|4:5]]\) and is mentioned in connection with God's faithfulness and His promises \([[Exod.6.8|Exodus 6:8]]; [[Exod.32.13|32:13]]; [[Exod.33.1|33:1]]; [[Lev.26.42|Leviticus 26:42]]\). In the New Testament, Abraham is upheld as an example of faith \([[Rom.4.1|Romans 4:1-3]], [[Rom.4.9|9]], [[Rom.4.12|12]], [[Rom.4.16|16]]; [[Gal.3.6|Galatians 3:6-9]], [[Gal.3.14|14]], [[Gal.3.29|29]]; [[Heb.11.8|Hebrews 11:8-19]]\) and is identified as the spiritual father of all believers \([[Rom.4.11|Romans 4:11-12]], [[Rom.4.16|16-17]]; [[Gal.3.7|Galatians 3:7]], [[Gal.3.29|29]]\). 

Jesus Christ is identified as the ultimate fulfillment of God's promises to Abraham \([[Matt.1.1|Matthew 1:1]]; [[Gal.3.16|Galatians 3:16]]\). Through faith in Christ, believers from all nations become part of Abraham's spiritual lineage and heirs to the promises made to him \([[Gal.3.7|Galatians 3:7-9]], [[Gal.3.14|14]], [[Gal.3.26|26-29]]\).
