Laban was the son of Bethuel and the brother of Rebekah, Isaac's wife \([[Gen.24.29|Gen.24.29]]\). He lived in Paddan Aram, where he welcomed Abraham's servant who sought a wife for Isaac \([[Gen.24.29|Gen.24.29-51]]\). Years later, Laban hosted his nephew Israel \(or 'Jacob'\), who fled from his brother Esau \([[Gen.27.43|Gen.27.43]]; [[Gen.28.2|28.2]],[[Gen.28.5|5]]\). Laban arranged for Jacob to marry his daughters, Leah and Rachel, in exchange for fourteen years of labor \([[Gen.29|Gen.29]]\). Laban also gave his maidservants, Zilpah and Bilhah, to Jacob as wives \([[Gen.30.9|Gen.30.9]],[[Gen.30.22|22]]\). Jacob's wives bore him eleven sons and a daughter while living with Laban \([[Gen.29.31|Gen.29.31-30.24]]; [[Gen.46.18|46.18]],[[Gen.46.25|25]]\). Laban and Jacob had a tense relationship due to Laban's deception and Jacob's prosperity \([[Gen.30|Gen.30-31]]\). After Jacob fled with his family, Laban pursued them but ultimately made a covenant with Jacob before departing \([[Gen.31|Gen.31]]\). Laban is last mentioned when Jacob sent messengers to Esau, stating that he had stayed with Laban \([[Gen.32.4|Gen.32.4]]\).
