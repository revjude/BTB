<small>*Carmel • Town in Judah; home of Nabal and Abigail*</small>

<span class='tipnrhighlight'>Carmel</span> was a town in Judah, known as the home of Nabal and his wife Abigail, who later married David.

Carmel, also known as Racal, was a town located in the hill country of Judah \([[Josh.15.55|Joshua 15:55]]\). It is mentioned several times in the Bible, particularly in relation to David's life. Carmel was the home of Nabal, a wealthy but foolish man who refused to assist David and his men \([[1Sam.25.2|1 Samuel 25:2-11]]\). Nabal's wife, Abigail, intervened and provided for David's needs, demonstrating her wisdom and resourcefulness \([[1Sam.25.14|1 Samuel 25:14-35]]\). 

After Nabal's death, David married Abigail, and she became one of his wives \([[1Sam.25.40|1 Samuel 25:40-42]]\). Later, when David was fleeing from Saul, he sent his two wives, Ahinoam and Abigail, to stay in Carmel \([[1Sam.27.3|1 Samuel 27:3]]\). The town is also mentioned in the list of places that received spoils from David's victory over the Amalekites \([[1Sam.30.29|1 Samuel 30:29]]\). 

In the Song of Solomon, the beauty of the bride's head is compared to Mount Carmel \([[Song.7.5|Song of Solomon 7:5]]\), likely referring to the lush vegetation and majesty of the mountain. The prophets Isaiah, Jeremiah, Amos, and Nahum use Carmel as a symbol of fertility, beauty, and God's judgment \([[Isa.33.9|Isaiah 33:9]], [[Isa.35.2|35:2]]; [[Jer.46.18|Jeremiah 46:18]], [[Jer.50.19|50:19]]; [[Amos.1.2|Amos 1:2]], [[Amos.9.3|9:3]]; [[Nah.1.4|Nahum 1:4]]\).
