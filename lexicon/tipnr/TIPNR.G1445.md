The Hebrews were an ethnic group descended from Abraham, also known as Israelites or Jews. The term "Hebrew" first appears in the Bible in reference to Abraham \([[Gen.14.13|Gen.14.13]]\) and is later used to refer to his descendants, the Israelites, throughout the Old Testament. The Hebrews were distinct from the Egyptians \([[Gen.43.32|Gen.43.32]]; [[Exod.1.15|Exo.1.15]],[[Exod.1.16|16]],[[Exod.1.19|19]]\) and other nations, and they were often oppressed or enslaved \([[Exod.1.11|Exo.1.11-14]]; [[Exod.5.3|5.3]]\). God identified Himself as the God of the Hebrews \([[Exod.3.18|Exo.3.18]]; [[Exod.5.3|5.3]]; [[Exod.7.16|7.16]]; [[Exod.9.1|9.1]],[[Exod.9.13|13]]; [[Exod.10.3|10.3]]\) and gave them laws and commandments to follow \([[Exod.21.2|Exo.21.2]]; [[Deut.15.12|Deu.15.12]]\). The Hebrews were also known for their language, Hebrew \([[Luke.23.38|Luk.23.38]]; [[Acts.6.1|Act.6.1]]\), and their religious practices. In the New Testament, the term "Hebrew" is used to refer to Jewish Christians who spoke Hebrew or Aramaic \([[Acts.6.1|Act.6.1]]\) and to emphasize the Jewish heritage of some early Christians \([[2Cor.11.22|2Co.11.22]]; [[Phil.3.5|Php.3.5]]\).
