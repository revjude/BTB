Caleb, the son of Jephunneh, was one of the twelve spies sent by Moses to explore the land of Canaan \([[Num.13.6|Num. 13:6]]\). Along with Joshua, Caleb stood firm in his faith and trust in God's promise, encouraging the Israelites to enter the land despite the other spies' negative report \([[Num.13.30|Num. 13:30]]; [[Num.14.6|14:6-9]]\). As a result, God commended Caleb for his faithfulness and promised that he would inherit the land he had explored \([[Num.14.24|Num. 14:24]], [[Num.14.30|30]], [[Num.14.38|38]]; [[Deut.1.36|Deut. 1:36]]\). After the conquest of Canaan, Caleb requested and received Hebron as his inheritance \([[Josh.14.6|Josh. 14:6-15]]; [[Josh.15.13|15:13-19]]; [[Josh.21.12|21:12]]; [[Judg.1.12|Judg. 1:12-15]], [[Judg.1.20|20]]\). He drove out the Anakim giants from Hebron and offered his daughter Achsah in marriage to whoever conquered Kirjath Sepher \([[Josh.15.14|Josh. 15:14-17]]; [[Judg.1.12|Judg. 1:12-13]]\). Caleb's descendants settled in the Negev region and in the cities of Hebron \([[1Sam.30.14|1 Sam. 30:14]]; [[1Chr.4.15|1 Chr. 4:15]]; [[1Chr.6.56|6:56]]\). His faith, courage, and obedience to God made him a prominent figure in Israel's history, and his story is remembered as an example of unwavering trust in God's promises \([[Num.32.12|Num. 32:12]]; [[Judg.3.9|Judg. 3:9]]\).
