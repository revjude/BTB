Joseph was the eleventh son of Israel \(or 'Jacob'\) and the firstborn of Rachel \([[Gen.30.22|Genesis 30:22-24]]\). He was favored by his father, causing jealousy among his brothers \([[Gen.37.3|Genesis 37:3-4]]\). Joseph had dreams of his family bowing down to him, further straining the relationships \([[Gen.37.5|Genesis 37:5-11]]\). His brothers sold him into slavery, and he was taken to Egypt \([[Gen.37.12|Genesis 37:12-36]]\). In Egypt, Joseph served in Potiphar's house, where he was falsely accused and imprisoned \([[Gen.39|Genesis 39]]\). While in prison, Joseph interpreted the dreams of Pharaoh's cupbearer and baker \([[Gen.40|Genesis 40]]\). Later, he interpreted Pharaoh's dreams and was appointed as second-in-command over Egypt \([[Gen.41.1|Genesis 41:1-45]]\). During this time, he was given the name Zaphenath-paneah by Pharaoh \([[Gen.41.45|Genesis 41:45]]\). Joseph managed Egypt's resources wisely during the years of plenty and famine \([[Gen.41.46|Genesis 41:46-57]]\). His brothers came to Egypt for food, and after testing them, Joseph revealed his identity and reconciled with them \([[Gen.42|Genesis 42-45]]\). Joseph brought his father, Jacob, and his entire family to Egypt, where they settled in the land of Goshen \([[Gen.46|Genesis 46-47]]\). Before his death, Jacob blessed Joseph's sons, Ephraim and Manasseh, and prophesied about Joseph's descendants \([[Gen.48|Genesis 48-49]]\). Joseph died at the age of 110 and was embalmed and placed in a coffin in Egypt \([[Gen.50.22|Genesis 50:22-26]]\). His bones were later carried out of Egypt by the Israelites during the Exodus and buried at Shechem \([[Exod.13.19|Exodus 13:19]]; [[Josh.24.32|Joshua 24:32]]\). Joseph's life exemplified faith, forgiveness, and God's providence in the midst of hardship.
