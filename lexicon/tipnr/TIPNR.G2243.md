Elijah was a prominent prophet in Israel during the reigns of King Ahab and his son Ahaziah \([[1Kgs.17|1Ki.17-2]]Ki.2\). He is introduced as a Tishbite from Gilead, who proclaimed a drought in Israel as a judgment from God \([[1Kgs.17.1|1Ki.17.1]]\). During the drought, God miraculously provided for Elijah through ravens and a widow in Zarephath \([[1Kgs.17.2|1Ki.17.2-16]]\). Elijah famously confronted the prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel, demonstrating the power of the true God and exposing the false prophets \([[1Kgs.18|1Ki.18]]\). He also confronted King Ahab and Queen Jezebel for their wickedness, particularly in the case of Naboth's vineyard \([[1Kgs.21|1Ki.21]]\). Elijah anointed Elisha as his successor and was taken up to heaven in a whirlwind, leaving behind his mantle for Elisha \([[2Kgs.2|2Ki.2]]\). He is mentioned in the New Testament as a forerunner to John the Baptist \([[Matt.11.14|Mat.11.14]]; [[Luke.1.17|Luk.1.17]]\) and as appearing with Moses at Jesus' transfiguration \([[Matt.17.3|Mat.17.3-4]]; [[Mark.9.4|Mrk.9.4-5]]; [[Luke.9.30|Luk.9.30-33]]\). Elijah is also referenced as an example of powerful prayer \([[Jas.5.17|Jas.5.17-18]]\) and as a prophet whom God had preserved a remnant of faithful Israelites \([[Rom.11.2|Rom.11.2-4]]\). The prophet Malachi foretold that Elijah would return before the coming of the Messiah \([[Mal.4.5|Mal.4.5-6]]\).
