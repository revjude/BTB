<small>*Macedonia • A region in northern Greece; Paul preached there*</small>

<span class='tipnrhighlight'>Macedonia</span>, a region in northern Greece where Paul preached the gospel and established churches \([[Acts.16.9|Acts 16:9-12]]; [[Acts.20.1|20:1-3]]\).
- A location first mentioned at [[Acts.16.9|Act.16.9]]; 
- referred to as Macedonia **<big>[[G3109|Μακεδονία]]</big>**, or Macedonia or Macedonian **<big>[[G3110|Μακεδών]]</big>**.<br>

Macedonia was a region located in northern Greece, which played a significant role in the early spread of Christianity. The apostle Paul received a vision of a man from Macedonia calling him to come and help them \([[Acts.16.9|Acts 16:9]]\), leading Paul and his companions to travel to Macedonia and preach the gospel there \([[Acts.16.10|Acts 16:10-12]]\). Paul visited several cities in Macedonia, including Philippi, Thessalonica, and Berea \([[Acts.16|Acts 16-17]]\). He later revisited Macedonia during his third missionary journey \([[Acts.20.1|Acts 20:1-3]]\). The churches in Macedonia, particularly the Philippians and Thessalonians, were known for their faith, generosity, and support of Paul's ministry \([[2Cor.8.1|2 Corinthians 8:1-5]]; [[Phil.4.15|Philippians 4:15-16]]; [[1Thess.1.7|1 Thessalonians 1:7-8]]\). Paul wrote epistles to the Philippians and Thessalonians, which are included in the New Testament. Macedonia is also mentioned in connection with Paul's travel plans \([[Rom.15.26|Romans 15:26]]; [[1Cor.16.5|1 Corinthians 16:5]]; [[2Cor.1.16|2 Corinthians 1:16]]\) and his co-workers, such as Sopater, Aristarchus, and Secundus \([[Acts.19.29|Acts 19:29]]; [[Acts.20.4|20:4]]; [[Acts.27.2|27:2]]\).
