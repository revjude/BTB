Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam, was appointed governor of Judah by the Babylonians after the fall of Jerusalem in 586 BC \([[2Kgs.25.22|2 Kings 25:22]]; [[Jer.40.5|Jeremiah 40:5]]\). He established his base at Mizpah and encouraged the remaining Jews to submit to Babylonian rule and live peacefully in the land \([[Jer.40.9|Jeremiah 40:9-10]]\). Many Jews who had fled to surrounding countries returned to Judah and settled under Gedaliah's leadership \([[Jer.40.11|Jeremiah 40:11-12]]\). However, Ishmael, a member of the royal family, conspired against Gedaliah and assassinated him along with the Babylonian officials and Jews who were with him \([[2Kgs.25.25|2 Kings 25:25]]; [[Jer.41.1|Jeremiah 41:1-3]]\). This act of treachery led to the flight of the remaining Jews to Egypt, fearing Babylonian reprisals \([[2Kgs.25.26|2 Kings 25:26]]; [[Jer.41.16|Jeremiah 41:16-18]]; [[Jer.43.5|43:5-7]]\). Gedaliah's assassination marked the end of Jewish self-rule in the land and the beginning of the Babylonian exile. The prophet Jeremiah, who had been released from prison by the Babylonians and entrusted to Gedaliah's care \([[Jer.39.14|Jeremiah 39:14]]; [[Jer.40.5|40:5-6]]\), witnessed these tragic events and mourned the loss of Gedaliah and the fate of his people \([[Jer.41.4|Jeremiah 41:4-6]]\).
