Man living at the time of Exile and Return

<span class='tipnrhighlight'>Rehum</span> was a Persian official who wrote to King Artaxerxes, attempting to stop the rebuilding of Jerusalem.
- A man living at the time of Exile and Return, first mentioned at [[Ezra.4.8|Ezr.4.8]]; 
- only referred to as Rehum **<big>[[H7348|רְחוּם]]</big>**.

Rehum is mentioned in the book of Ezra as a high-ranking Persian official who held the title of "commander" \([[Ezra.4.8|Ezra 4:8]], [[Ezra.4.9|9]], [[Ezra.4.17|17]], [[Ezra.4.23|23]]\). When the enemies of the Jews learned that they had begun rebuilding the temple and the city walls of Jerusalem, they wrote a letter to King Artaxerxes to persuade him to stop the work \([[Ezra.4.7|Ezra 4:7-16]]\). Rehum was one of the officials who signed this letter, along with Shimshai the scribe and others \([[Ezra.4.9|Ezra 4:9]]\). In the letter, they accused the Jews of rebuilding a rebellious and wicked city that had a history of causing trouble for kings \([[Ezra.4.12|Ezra 4:12-16]]\). As a result, Artaxerxes ordered the work to be stopped until further notice \([[Ezra.4.17|Ezra 4:17-22]]\). Rehum and his associates then went to Jerusalem and forcibly made the Jews stop building \([[Ezra.4.23|Ezra 4:23]]\). This opposition delayed the completion of the temple and the city walls until the reign of King Darius \([[Ezra.4.24|Ezra 4:24]]\). Rehum's actions demonstrate the political challenges faced by the Jews as they sought to rebuild Jerusalem after returning from Babylonian exile.
