Eliphaz the Temanite was one of the three friends who visited Job during his time of suffering \([[Job.2.11|Job.2.11]]\). He is described as coming from Teman, an area known for its wisdom \([[Jer.49.7|Jer.49.7]]\). Eliphaz, along with Bildad and Zophar, engaged in a series of dialogues with Job, attempting to explain the reasons behind Job's afflictions \([[Job.4|Job.4-25]]\). In his speeches, Eliphaz emphasizes the justice and sovereignty of God, suggesting that Job's suffering is a result of sin and urging him to repent \([[Job.4.1|Job.4.1]]; [[Job.15.1|15.1]]; [[Job.22.1|22.1]]\). However, at the end of the book, God rebukes Eliphaz and his two friends for not speaking what is right about Him, unlike Job \([[Job.42.7|Job.42.7]]\). God instructs them to offer sacrifices and have Job pray for them to be forgiven \([[Job.42.9|Job.42.9]]\). The Bible does not provide any further information about Eliphaz's life outside of his interactions with Job.
