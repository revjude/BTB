Lot was the son of Haran and the nephew of Abraham \([[Gen.11.27|Gen.11.27]],[[Gen.11.31|31]]\). He traveled with Abraham from Ur to Canaan \([[Gen.12.4|Gen.12.4-5]]\). Due to strife between their herdsmen, Lot chose to settle in the plain of Jordan near Sodom \([[Gen.13.1|Gen.13.1-13]]\). Lot was captured when Sodom was invaded, but Abraham rescued him \([[Gen.14.12|Gen.14.12]],[[Gen.14.16|16]]\). Despite the wickedness of Sodom, Lot continued living there \([[Gen.19.1|Gen.19.1]]\). When God revealed His plan to destroy the city, Abraham pleaded for Lot's safety \([[Gen.18.16|Gen.18.16-33]]\). Two angels warned Lot to flee Sodom with his family \([[Gen.19.1|Gen.19.1-22]]\). Lot's wife disobeyed and was turned into a pillar of salt \([[Gen.19.26|Gen.19.26]]; [[Luke.17.32|Lk.17.32]]\). Lot and his daughters escaped to Zoar and later settled in a cave \([[Gen.19.30|Gen.19.30]]\). Lot's daughters, believing they were the last survivors, got him drunk and slept with him, resulting in the births of Moab and Ammon \([[Gen.19.31|Gen.19.31-38]]; [[Ps.83.8|Ps.83.8]]\). The New Testament describes Lot as a righteous man distressed by the debauchery of Sodom \([[2Pet.2.7|2Pe.2.7]]\). Jesus used Lot's story as a warning about the suddenness of His return \([[Luke.17.28|Lk.17.28-29]]\).
